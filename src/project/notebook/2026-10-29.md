_Settling, impact sensitivity and cohesion experiments run while the first
first night ran on the lab Mac. Beds: `thick` = 1.5 m × 1.5 m, 0.9 m of
bed-1 boulders (116 grains; 12 vertices, friction 0.6, dt 2 ms, cap
0.3 m/s, full NLGS budget unless noted); `3 m` = 3 m × 3 m, same layer
(463 grains). Drops on the 2.4 m `smoke` bed (boulders + thin top layer of small stones) with
the 12 kg box with its panels folded. Raw folders are not kept; the numbers are._

rest with the speed limit on** (step 20,600: 0 grains moving; 21,000: 54 at

`gap/dt` ejection of residual penetrations: a smaller dt kicks harder. The
3 m bed with the 1,000-iteration deposition budget and the speed limit kept rested

trigger like a cap lift; nothing may change between phases.

## Impact sensitivity (same drop, two conditions)

| rock–rock arm | A: 5 cm/s, 20° | B: 8 cm/s, 30° |
|---|---|---|
| baseline (friction 0.6, restitution 0.5) | detached, e 0.400 | left_patch, e 0.313 |
| restitution 0.3 / 0.7 | 0.399 / 0.401 | 0.352 / 0.309 |
| friction 0.4 / 0.7 | left_patch 0.157 / 0.433 | 0.222 / 0.361 |
| cohesive 10 Pa / 100 Pa | 0.455 / 0.457 | 0.372 / 0.366 |

Reading: the rock–rock restitution does not matter for the first bounce
(the craft–rock pair does); rock–rock friction matters a lot (0.4 lets the
box slide out of the patch; 0.7 returns it faster); cohesion stiffens the
bed (+14 % restitution) and saturates — 10 Pa and 100 Pa are the same bed
for a 12 kg craft at cm/s, because 10 Pa over a boulder face is already a
thousand times the craft's weight at Dimorphos. Condition B needs a patch
larger than 0.5 m (most arms left the patch): the 36-drop set' 1.2 m patch on
the 6 m beds is sized for this. Cohesive runs carry 238–274 bonded
(`Mstck`) contacts; the baseline none.

## Does the settle instability reach the impact decks? No.

Impact decks use the speed rule (`RST_CLB`) for rock–rock pairs, start
from the settled geometry with velocities zeroed, at 5e-5 m/s². In the
baseline drop the 108 free grains moved a median 0.13 µm (max 14 µm) during
the 0.42 s of free flight before the craft touched anything; by the end of
the 5.4 s run (after the bounce) the median was 0.35 mm and the maximum
18.6 mm. The gap-law ejection that destabilises a settling pile does not
exist under the speed rule, and the pipeline saves a bed the moment its
rest test passes — the metastability seen in the long hold runs is a
settle-phase phenomenon only.

## Gravity self-similarity (physics check)

The same drop at g = 2.5e-5 / 5e-5 / 1e-4 m/s² with the arrival speed scaled
by √g (0.0354 / 0.05 / 0.0707 m/s): e = 0.3997 / 0.3995 / 0.3995. Rigid
cohesionless contact dynamics is self-similar in gravity to 0.1 % here, so a
Froude-scaled result transfers across Dimorphos' 4.4–5.5e-5 m/s² range and
the time step rule holds.

## Cohesion gate (D10)

Scene `cohesion` (tests/sim): the Mohr–Coulomb bond is a stress times the
detected contact area, needs `coht = cohn`, works between polyhedra only
(plane contacts have no area). Cohesive rock–rock laws for impact decks:
`configs/laws/regolith_cohesive_10pa.toml`, `_100pa.toml`.

## Stability runs (thick bed, rest required for 20,000 steps)

| lever | rest reached | then |

| dt 3 ms / 4 ms (tol 1e-3) | 2,700 / 3,300 | exploded within a few hundred steps of rest |

| baseline, second run (tol 1e-3) | 5,000 | **held all 20,000 steps** — at 1e-3 the hold is stochastic (one run of two) |

| 4 m bed, seed 2 (tol 1e-3, standard window) | 9,400 | exploded in the window like seed 1 (9,200); snapshot used, QA WARN on `rest_window`; 0.59 s/step |
| 4 m bed at tolerance 1e-4, seeds 1–2 | not reached by the shutdown (steps 8,400 / 9,200 after 6.4 h; the 1e-3 runs rested at 9,200 / 9,400) | the cost ratio is 4–5× at this size (1.2 s/step early, 2.5–2.7 s/step as the pile packs, against 0.55–0.59 at 1e-3); whether the window holds at 4 m is still open — `configs/experiments/4m_tol4.toml`, ~8 h on the laptop |

In every exploding case the settle **fell back to its first-rest snapshot**
and finished as a usable bed (`rest_window: WARN`, every other QA check
PASS) — the behaviour the study beds now rely on.

The NLGS tolerance is the lever, and it confirms the mechanism: residual
penetrations within the tolerance are what the position rule keeps ejecting at
gap/dt, and a ten-times tighter tolerance makes the kicks small enough for
friction to absorb. On the 1.5 m test bed it cost ~8× per step and ~1.4×
the steps to rest; on the 3 m bed (463 boulders, four grains deep) the
ratio is only ~3× (875 ms/step against 150–300 ms/step at 1e-3 for the
same count, depending on the laptop's load; 1.9 vs 0.7 ms per body-step),
because the full solve at 1e-3 already runs hundreds of iterations on a
deep pile — and that bed held its standard window and passed QA with no
WARN, where the 4 m bed at 1e-3 exploded inside the window for both
seeds and lives on its first-rest snapshot. So the study rule stays
(tolerance 1e-3, rest test, 500-step 500-step check, save
at once, change nothing between phases; impact decks are immune, above),
and tolerance 1e-4 is the paid option for a bed that must hold its window
without the snapshot, be held, or be built on (a second layer deposited
later, a long micro-g relaxation). The 4 m bed at 1e-4 (two seeds) is
running to say whether the ratio and the hold carry to that size.
(2026-10-30: every window in this table ran *uncapped* — see the correction
above — so "exploded in the window" here means "exploded once the cap was
lifted". `configs/experiments/3m_capwin.toml` and `4m_capwin.toml` repeat
the 3 m and 4 m beds with the speed limit kept and the 3 mm/s rest. The 3 m bed:
first rest at step 4,300, 500-step check held, converged at 4,800, QA PASS with no
WARN — 17 min on the laptop. The 4 m bed: first rest at 4,700, 500-step check held,
QA PASS — where both uncapped seeds had exploded. Results in docs/BED_STUDY.md and the bed
cards under reports/laptop/beds/.)

## Small-stone layers (bed-4 risk)

A 2 m bed with 0.5 m of boulders (327) and a 10 cm top layer of small stones 8–16 cm long
(1,415): both stages converged (boulders 7,100 steps at 151 ms/step; small stones
5,100 steps at 588 ms/step — 10,460 contacts, 4.6× the boulder stage) and
the bed **failed QA**: small stones overlap 0.84 of the smallest small stones semi-axis
(~20 mm) under the speed rule (`fines_law = "RST_CLB"`). The smoke bed's
3 cm top layer warns at 0.2–0.4; a thicker small-stone layer drifts further. Beds
4–6 carry the same law. The position rule for small-stone pairs (`fines_law =
"GAP_SGR_CLB"`) is being tested on the same bed; its known cost is small stones
draining through the boulder pores for thousands of steps, which the
rest test may now tolerate. Result appended below.

**Small-stones-only pair (1 m bed, 353 small stones 8–16 cm long on the floor, no
boulders).** Speed rule: rest at 1,000 steps, 19 ms/step, worst
stone–stone overlap 7.8 mm (0.32 of the smallest small stones semi-axis, WARN).
Position rule: rest at 1,300 steps, 51 ms/step (2.7×), overlap 0.0 mm, QA WARN
only for 2 % bare floor. So the position rule does for small stones what it does for
boulders — the overlap is the speed rule's, not the small stones' shape — at
~3× the step cost on a thin layer.

**Layered bed under the position rule** (the same 2 m bed, 327 boulders +
1,415 small stones): stopped at the laptop's shutdown after 5,800 steps and
9.4 h of the small-stone stage, **not at rest**. The step cost rose from 4.0 to
5.8 s (10× the speed rule's 0.59 s/step on the same bed; 3.3 ms per
body-step, 4× the position rule's price on a small-stones-only pile), 950–1,400 of
1,742 grains were moving at every check, and the median displacement per
second fell from 23 mm (step 1,000) to ~10 mm (step 3,500) and stayed
there (9–11 mm through step 5,800; the rest test asks < 1 mm). No overlap
(min gap 0.0), no escape: small stones wedged between boulders flicker under the
position rule the way deep boulder piles do, and here the flicker does not die.
So the position rule for *every* small-stone pair is out for beds 4–6: ~3 ms per
body-step for days on the lab Mac, and it may never rest.

**Where the overlap actually is.** On the velocity-law layered bed the
worst overlaps by pair type are boulder–stone **−20.2 mm** (2,719 pairs),
stone–stone −8.1 mm (5,124 pairs), small stones–floor −0.5 mm: the FAIL (0.84 of
the 24 mm smallest small stones semi-axis) is the boulder–stone pairs; stone–stone
sit at 0.34, the WARN level of the small-stones-only beds, which pass. The next
experiment is therefore a split: position rule for boulder–stone pairs (to a
fine a boulder is a wall, and the position rule pushes it out instead of letting
it sink 20 mm), speed rule among small stones (no draining, no flicker).
`[settle] boulder_fines_law = "GAP_SGR_CLB"` does that (added 2026-10-30;
at its default the key does not enter bed ids, so nothing moved);
`configs/experiments/fines_split.toml` is the 2 m layered bed with it,
~1–2 h on the laptop. Beds 4–6 wait for its answer.

**Thick small-stones-only pair (1 m × 1 m bed, 25 cm of small stones 8–16 cm long, 882 grains —
bed 6's top layer, and bed 5's bottom layer in grain depth).** Speed rule:
rest at 1,400 steps, 70 ms/step, worst overlap 8.5 mm (0.35, WARN) — a
small-stone pile on its own stays under the FAIL limit; it is small stones poured over
boulders that fail. Position rule: rest at 3,500 (2.5× the steps), 656 ms/step
(9.4×), overlap 0.0 mm, QA PASS, and a denser pile (surface 0.222 m
against 0.259 m). At 0.74 ms per body-step the position rule costs the same on
these small stones as on the 4 m bed's boulders (0.67–0.71): its price is the
deep pile's, not the grain's — ~0.7 ms per body-step once a pile is
several grains deep, against ~0.08 for the speed rule. Total wall: 2 min
against 55 min.

## The study configuration, rehearsed end to end (4 m bed)

A 4 m version of bed 1 (821 boulders) settled under the snapshot code:
first rest at step 9,200, the 500-step check exploded by 9,600, the
first-rest snapshot became the bed — QA PASS, 88 min on a loaded laptop
(~0.55 s/step with the full solver). Then the standard-drop configuration
exactly as the lab Mac runs it (Milani with panels open, fixed pose, adjacency
shell, 300 s cap; 1.0 m patch and ±0.2 m sites because the bed is 4 m, not
6 m): 8 drops on 4 workers in 5 min, 7 detached + 1 `left_patch`, QA PASS
(one WARN), 25–267 s per run with 107–132 free and 209–239 fixed grains.
e ranged 0.16–0.67 at 0° and 0.41–0.67 at 40° across four spots 0.4 m
apart — the landing spot again outweighs everything else.

A second seed of the same 4 m bed (827 boulders) behaved the same way:
first rest at 9,400, the window exploded, the snapshot became the bed, QA
WARN on `rest_window` and nothing else, 0.59 s/step. At 4 m the window
explodes every time at tolerance 1e-3, so the study beds will carry
that WARN unless they are settled at 1e-4 (held at 3 m; 4 m under test).

## Vertex count on the impact side

The same drop on smoke beds generated with 8 / 12 / 20-vertex grains:
e = 0.165 (left the patch, first contact on small stones) / 0.400 / 0.506 (both
first contact on a boulder). A different vertex count is a different random
bed, so this spread is the landing spot's geometry and what the craft hits
first — and it is larger than any contact-law arm: terrain variability
dominates the outcome, which is the position study's premise. The 8-vertex
bed also took ~3× the steps of the 12-vertex one to settle.
