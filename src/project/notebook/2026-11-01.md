# 31 October, night: the exact last contact, the craft's centre of mass

Laptop. Settling in the background: the 2 m fines bed (`bed4q_2m`, its 12
drops queued) and the 3 m veneer bed (`bed4u_3m`, its 36 drops queued).

## How do we know the last contact is the last?

The question behind every "bounced off". After its last touch the craft is
in free flight, and a free body feels only gravity, which exerts no torque:
its centre of mass follows a ballistic arc and its rotation obeys Euler's
equations for a torque-free body (the spin axis wobbles because the three
moments of inertia differ). LMGC90 integrates the same equations, with
the gyroscopic torque as an explicit term (`comp_Fext_RBDY3`). So the
craft's future after a drop is known exactly, and can be checked against
the whole bed rather than predicted.

`src/milani/flight.py` integrates it (RK4, steps of at most 0.02 s) and
was first compared with the simulation's own recorded free flights: over
12 drops of bed 1 with 7-70 s of free flight each, the predicted centre
stays within 0.001 mm and the orientation within 0.06 degrees of
LMGC90's. On one drop, every point of the craft's surface is within
0.015 mm after 48 s.

The flight is moved over the bed in two stages. The bed is kept as three
layers: the crop's free grains, the frozen ring, and the grains outside
the simulation.

1. **Coarse:** points every 2 cm on the craft's surface are compared with
   each layer's top surface on the 1 cm grid, each cell raised to its
   highest neighbour.
2. **Exact:** wherever a point comes within 2 cm, the exact distance
   between the craft's convex pieces and the nearby grains' convex
   polyhedra decides. That distance is the closest pair of points of two
   convex hulls, a small least-squares problem; on 1000 pairs of boxes with
   known distances its worst error is 2e-13 m. Near a grain, every step is
   the longest in which the craft cannot close the distance (conservative
   advancement), so no touch falls between two tests.

A touch is an exact distance under 1 mm.

Two things nearly went wrong on the way:

- scipy's `nnls` fails to converge on 10-15 % of touching boxes. Bounded
  least squares (`lsq_linear`, BVLS) fails on none and takes 0.15 ms.
- The `divide by zero / overflow encountered in matmul` warnings that
  appeared with the first version are a numpy 2.0 + Apple Accelerate
  artefact. The products are exact (4e-16 from a reference).

## The craft turned about the wrong point

The first exact check said drop b955841420ca (bed 1) would touch a free
grain 4 s after its run had ended. Re-simulated past that moment, it never
did, and the solver's own gap to that boulder never went below 2.3 mm
(9.4 mm at the predicted moment). So the two geometries disagreed. Placing
the craft from craft.json next to the craft in the run's ParaView frames
showed a constant offset of 10.172 mm along the bus x axis, at every frame.

The cause is in LMGC90's reader (mod_RBDY3, `read_bodies`). A body made of
several contactors is centred on the volume-weighted centroid of their
geometric volumes, while the principal inertia it is given is kept. The
pre side sets each piece's "effective volume" from its mass, and LMGC90
never sees it: it recomputes every polyhedron's volume from its vertices.
The deployed craft is a 10.8 kg bus and two 1 cm, 0.6 kg wings, far lighter
for their volume. In every deployed drop so far it therefore turned about
a point 10.2 mm from its centre of mass, toward the wings. The stowed box
(one piece) was right.

The fix (`impactors.ballast_piece`) puts a box inside the bus, 3.9 x 24 x
36 cm and 3 mm inside every wall, so that no grain can reach it before the
bus. Its volume moves the geometric centroid exactly onto the centre of
mass, and the density becomes 12 kg / total geometric volume. In pre the
ballast must be massless. The first attempt gave it zero inertia, and pre
treats any inertia below 1e-6 as "not given" and computes both volume and
inertia from the geometry, so the ballast came out heavy: the centre moved
7.17 mm and the inertia grew 7 %. It now gets 2e-6, and the bus 2e-6 less;
`build_avatar` asserts that pre's centre and inertia are the intended ones.

Checked in a run: the simulated craft sits within 1.6e-11 m of its design,
with inertia 0.4535 / 0.1531 / 0.3387 kg m^2 (intended 0.4534 / 0.1531 /
0.3386). Every drop now records this check (`setup.craft_geometry_error_m`)
and warns above 0.1 mm. On one 4 m-bed drop the fix moved e from 0.300 to
0.329 and the last touch from 3.50 to 3.78 s.

Re-checked with the craft where LMGC90 really had it, the 93 bounced-off
drops made before tonight:

## A drop now ends on its exact flight

When the craft has been free for 0.5 s and is rising, its flight is
scanned. The drop goes on while the flight comes within 1 cm, plus the
fastest free grain's surface speed times the time ahead, of a grain the
simulation can move: the solver decides those, also when the craft's
centre has already crossed the crop's edge but a wing is still over it.
Otherwise the drop is over:

- `clear`: no touch on the bed in the time the run could still take;
- `beyond`: the next touch is on the frozen ring or the bed outside the
  crop. The prediction is stored with the drop (`contact.flight`).

The grain-speed allowance came from the first re-simulation. One drop was
declared over 1 s after its last touch while passing 10.08 mm from a free
grain, and one second after an impact the grains are still moving.

This replaces both the circumsphere test of the evening and the
"airborne over the crop edge" rule. Results are identical where the
physics is unchanged (stowed craft: e to five digits), and drops end 0.5-1 s
after their last touch instead of tens of seconds later: a 4 m-bed drop
took 21 s of computing instead of 134 s.

`lab.py check-last-contact` now runs the same exact flight from the last
touch over the whole bed, seconds per drop:

- **Until the run's end:** only the grains outside the simulation are
  checked, because inside it the solver decided.
- **After the end:** all three layers are checked, the free grains at their
  final pose. Impact runs now write a ParaView frame at the very end for
  this.
- **The replay** (detachment off, 150 s more) runs where the flight passes
  within 5 cm of a free grain, because only there can the grains' own later
  motion matter; `--replay all` replays every drop.

Verdicts: confirmed, next touch off the patch, lands back on the patch,
touched again.

## Every laptop drop re-simulated

All 108 drops of the three laptop sets (bed 1, the 4 m bed, the 3 m Weibull
bed) were re-simulated in place with both fixes
(`lab.py campaign rerun NAME --stale`; the old records are in each run's
`superseded/`). Every first contact is identical to the original record
(same moment, piece and feature): the fixes act only after it. The simulated
craft sits within 1.6e-11 m of its design in every drop.

| set | craft | bounced off / left the patch | e at 0°, 4 / 8 cm/s | e at 40°, 4 / 8 cm/s | last contact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bed 1, 4.5 m, uniform boulders | deployed | 32 / 4 | 0.231 / 0.231 | 0.367 / 0.407 | 20 confirmed, 11 next touch off the patch, 1 lands back on it |
| 4 m, uniform boulders, spots 5 cm apart | deployed | 32 / 4 | 0.256 / 0.264 | 0.312 / 0.327 | 24 confirmed, 8 off the patch |
| 3 m, Weibull boulders | stowed | 32 / 4 | 0.339 / 0.343 | 0.533 / 0.531 | 30 confirmed, 2 off the patch |
| 2 m, boulders under an 8 cm fines veneer | stowed | 8 / 4 | 0.304 / 0.307 | 0.370 / 0.307 | 8 confirmed |

(medians; the 2 m set's 40° numbers include drops that left the patch.)

- **The stowed craft (3 m set) is unchanged** to the last digit of e, as it
  must be: one piece, so its centre was always right, and the new end test
  only ends drops sooner (median 7.6 -> 6.1 s simulated).
- **The deployed craft moved.** Medians shift by at most 0.02 (bed 1 at
  40 degrees: 0.371 -> 0.381; 4 m at 0 degrees: 0.243 -> 0.258), single
  drops by up to ±0.1, and in each deployed set 2 drops went from bounced
  off to left the patch and 2-4 the other way. A deployed landing is a
  3-4-touch tumble, and 1 cm of lever arm changes which wing comes down
  where.
- **No drop ended too early.** In the four drops where the end test had
  predicted the next touch on the frozen ring, the replay (end test off) ran
  on into that touch: predicted at 35.788, 72.507, 22.093 and 40.776 s,
  simulated at 35.835, 72.515, 22.130 and 40.835 s. That is the exact flight
  agreeing with the full simulation to 0.06 s or better.
- **Bounces that land just outside the patch:** 21 of 96 make their next
  touch on the frozen ring or the bed beyond, 2 within 6 s (at the patch
  edge, 1.24-1.31 m from the target), 9 within 10-60 s, 10 later. One bed-1
  hop comes back down on the patch, 0.15 m from the target, 518 s after its
  last touch (beyond the 300 s run limit).
- **Fines (2 m bed):** in all 12 drops the craft's first contact was a
  boulder. An 8 cm veneer of 4-8 cm grains does not cover boulders 10-25 cm
  across; their tops stick out. Straight down, the three approach directions
  are the same drop (the craft's pose does not turn with the direction), so
  the six vertical drops are two results repeated exactly. At 40 degrees the
  craft often slides out of the 0.45 m crop a 2 m bed allows.

Movies and figures were remade for all 120 drops and the reel rebuilt.
