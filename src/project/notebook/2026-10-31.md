# Laptop experiments, 2026-10-31 (while the lab Mac runs the weekend slate)

_What the laptop did on the Saturday: the standard drops on a settled 4 m
bed, twice, to shake out the impact machinery before the lab Mac's

## The 36 standard drops on the 4 m bed (`exp_4m_capwin`, bed-1 geometry)

Nine spots at ±0.05 m (all a 4 m bed allows with the 1.2 m crop), 4 and
8 cm/s, 0° and 40°, fixed pose. First set (`exp_drops_4m_capwin`): the
detector of 2026-10-30 for its first 12 runs, then two intermediate states
of the look-ahead. Second set (`exp_drops_4m_capwin_r2`): the final
detector. Same bed, same spots, same conditions.

| cell | first set, old detector | second set, final detector |
|---|---|---|
| 4 cm/s, 0° | 2 of 5 detached, 3 voided `left_patch`; e 0.21–0.24; 55–100 s simulated, 4–6 min wall each | 8 of 9 detached (1 left on the ground); e median 0.245; 14 s simulated, 68 s wall |
| 8 cm/s, 0° | 5 of 7 detached, 2 voided; e 0.23–0.26; 40–46 s simulated | 9 of 9 detached; e median 0.239; 7 s simulated, 28 s wall |
| 4 cm/s, 40° | all detached; e 0.33; 12 s simulated | all detached; e 0.330; 4 s simulated |
| 8 cm/s, 40° | all detached; e 0.33; 6 s simulated | all detached; e 0.337; 2.3 s simulated |

What the first set exposed, and what changed (DECISIONS 2026-10-31):

1. **Voided bounces.** Five vertical drops ended `left_patch`: the craft
   had bounced, was airborne and rising (0.7 cm/s) with 1.8 cm/s sideways,
   and drifted 15–26 s until a wingtip crossed the crop edge. The
   detachment test never passed because its look-ahead keeps the craft's
   vertices out of every grain's *bounding sphere*, and a 1.47 m craft
   20 cm above a boulder field sits inside those spheres until it has risen
   above the tallest one — a minute at micro-g. Now: the bed's height map
   is the obstacle (raised by the largest grain displacement since the
   impact), the sphere test remains as a second way to pass, and a craft
   crossing the edge airborne and rising is `detached` with the exit state
   of its last contact (`contact.detached_how = "patch_edge"`; 2 of the 36
   in the second set). `python3 lab.py campaign regrade` relabels old runs
   from their trajectories (3 of the 5; the other 2 left on the ground).
2. **First-touch detachment.** With the height map alone, the look-ahead
   cleared vertical drops 0.5 s after the first touch and reported e of
   0.31–0.35, where the old detector, following the 3–6-touch tumble of a
   slowly spinning craft, gave 0.21–0.26. The look-ahead translated the
   craft without its spin. Both tests now rotate the vertices along the
   path for at least half a turn (Rodrigues), and the second set reproduces
   the old e: the tumble is physics, the hovering afterwards was not.
3. **Solver warnings on short runs.** `solver_unconverged_fraction` warned
   on 14 of 36 runs — the ones that ended in a second. A run has a fixed
   handful of steps at the sweep budget (8–13: the crop's own relaxation
   before the touch and the shock of the touch), so the fraction of all
   steps read 4 % on a 1 s run and 0.06 % on a 100 s one. It is graded on
   the contact phase now (WARN above 5 %); the second set has one WARN
   left, from a run that started before the change.
4. **Campaigns could not name a library bed.** `bed = "@exp_4m_capwin"`
   failed at config load; the loader now resolves bed references through
   the library (unit test).
5. **Spot grid on a small bed.** The campaign's usable square is
   L/2 − crop − 2 r_max − 5 cm: ±0.05 m on 4 m, ±0.30 m on 4.5 m (the
   production grid fits exactly).

Everything else was clean on all 72 runs: first contact always on free
boulders, box penetration ≤ 0.14 of the smallest semi-axis, crop-edge
disturbance ≤ 0.04 of the impact speed (the 1.2 m crop is ample for the
deployed craft at these speeds), energy ratio ≤ 0.29, no crash, no stall.
Cost: the second set took 0.4 CPU-hours — 10–70 s per drop on the laptop.

  0.22–0.27 and 0.37–0.39), different beds though; its first contact is
  an edge or corner 24 times out of 36 where the deployed craft lands on
  a wing or a face. The pair on the fines veneer (`exp_drops_bed4_3m`,
  same boulders, same spots) follows.
- **Weibull piles rest slowly.** `exp_bed2_3m` (934 boulders 0.065–0.40 m,
  3 m) had a median move of 6–8 mm/s from step 5,100 to 7,900 and 4–6
  to 10,800, still above the 3 mm/s rest at 11,500 — where the uniform
  bed 1 at 4.5 m rested at 4,500. A deterministic twin with the extra
  columns (`exp_bed2_3m_b`, same bed, identical step for step) showed the
  load-bearing half moving as much as the rest (skeleton median 9.6 vs
  8.4, 12.3 vs 10.4 mm/s), so the "small rattlers" explanation is wrong:
  the whole pile churns in bursts (mean speed 2–6 cm/s, a third of the
  grains moving) under the gap law's kicks. It did rest in the end: first
  rest at ~13,700 steps (27 s simulated), window held, 15,100 steps and
  4.7 h on the shared laptop, QA PASS with no WARN — 3.4x the steps of the
  uniform bed. Consequences: the estimator budgets 2.5x the steps for a
  Weibull layer. `exp_bed2_3m_cap015` (the
  same bed with a 0.15 m/s cap) was stopped at 5,200 steps to free the
  laptop for the fines bed: its rain-down takes twice as long (median
  move 15 mm/s at 4,800 against 7.8 for the control) and whether the
  churn then ends sooner would have needed hours more — inconclusive.

## Movies of every drop, and a page to browse them (evening)

`lab.py movie` renders each drop's ParaView frames with ParaView 6.0's own
Python (offscreen) and encodes `movie.mp4`; `lab.py gallery` builds a page
with one tab per drop set, a card per drop (movie, key numbers) and a
detail view (every number of the record, the nine run figures). The 108
drops of bed 1, the 4 m detector check and the 3 m Weibull set are on it.
The campaign trim had deleted the frames of 94 of them; each was replayed
from its `job.json` in a scratch folder and **all 94 replays were
identical to the record** (outcome, step count and e to the last digit):
the impact runs are deterministic, so frames, or anything else a run did
not keep, can be recreated at will. Campaigns no longer trim `DISPLAY/`.

- The deployed craft does not bounce once: it lands on a wing (32 of 36
  first contacts on bed 1), tips, and leaves after 3–4 touches spread
  over seconds to tens of seconds. The stowed box hits an edge or corner
  and is gone within a second.
- 40° from vertical always keeps more speed than straight down (the
  tangential part survives), on every bed and for both craft.
- At 0° the spread of e between landing spots on bed 1 (0.07–0.42) is far
  larger than on the 4 m bed's tightly packed spots (0.21–0.30): the
  terrain under the craft is what varies the bounce.
- The fastest exits are 4.0 cm/s (bed 1) and 5.2 cm/s (stowed, Weibull);
  the grains barely move (fastest grain a few mm/s, a median of 13–27
  grains displaced by more than 1 mm).
