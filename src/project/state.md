# Where things stand

_Written 2 November 2026. The tables are regenerated from what the lab Mac pushes
to GitHub._

**The short version.** We simulate Hera's [Milani](../glossary.md#milani) CubeSat
landing on ground like [Dimorphos](../glossary.md#dimorphos)'s and measure how it
bounces. The study now has one job: settle [three standard beds](beds.md), run
[the same drops](results.md) on each, and compare.

{{#include generated/status.md}}

**What the lab Mac is doing.** It runs by itself. It settles the three beds (three
seeds each) and two small test beds. When all of a bed's seeds have settled, its
drops start, then the last-contact check, the movies, the reports and the
[drop reel](drop-reel.md). Everything is pushed to GitHub when a step ends, whether
it succeeded or failed, and every 2 hours while it runs.

**What we know so far:**

- **Bed 1's recipe is ready.** With the pour fixed, the same recipe at 2 m settled in
  about 70 minutes, passed the [quality checks](../glossary.md#quality-checks-qa)
  and tilted 2° instead of 9°.
- **Bed 2 is the uncertain one.** No bed with this many small stones among boulders
  has come to rest yet. Its small test tells us early how much of the surface is
  stones, how far the boulders stand out, and whether it settles. If the recipe
  needs changing, its three seeds restart.
- **Bed 3** is stones only, which the solver handles far faster. Earlier stone-only
  tests came to rest quickly, but it hasn't been tried at this size.
